# Cover rules: what makes someone stop scrolling

Round 6 feedback from Vish: the 3D object on a cover must say what the post is about, at a glance. Decorative objects get skipped. These rules apply to every first slide, single image and video's first frame.

1. **The hero acts out the headline.** Pick the one noun and verb of the hook and show that literally:
   - "sign texts invisibly" → a UV torch revealing a hidden signature on a chat bubble
   - "creator ads in one click" → a cursor clicking a button that turns a creator post into a "Sponsored" ad

   Test: hide the title. Can someone still guess the topic from the picture? If not, redo it.
2. **One context symbol.** Add a single symbol that places the story: a ring of gold stars for the EU, a feed card for social, a chat bubble for an AI chat app. Always generic shapes, never real logos.
3. **A face with an emotion.** Faces are the strongest scroll-stopper, and an emotion tells people how to feel about the news. Vish's 3D avatar gives the reaction:
   - surprised for news
   - a smirk for "here's the catch"
   - laughing on the call to action
   - full body, arms crossed, for opinion and creator-economy posts

   Dot stays on explainer slides.
4. **Point at the key thing.** A label chip with an arrow ("invisible watermark ↓", "Sponsored", "1 click") steers the eye to the payoff in under a second.
5. **Curiosity gap.** The headline promises a reveal ("Invisibly.") and the picture shows half of it. The swipe completes it.
6. **One focal point, high contrast.** One hero group, big. The title is never covered, and labels never cover the hero's key detail.
7. **Before and after, or cause and effect,** whenever the story is a change: arrow from old to new.

The QA reviewer checks rule 1 by describing the cover image without the title. That description must name the topic.

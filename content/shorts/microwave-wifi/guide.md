# Is your microwave slowing down your Wi-Fi? The 1-page guide

Read this in 3 minutes and you will know why your video buffers when the microwave runs, why it is safe, and the one setting that fixes it.
Sources linked under every point, checked 28 Oct 2026 · @buildwithvish · October 2026

## 1. The short version
Microwave ovens cook at about 2.45 GHz, right next to 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi. The door blocks almost all of it, but the tiny amount that legally leaks can still drown out your router nearby. Putting your devices on 5 GHz Wi-Fi avoids the problem.

## 2. Same stretch of airwaves
US rules list 2450 MHz (give or take 50 MHz) as an industrial, scientific and medical frequency, the band microwave ovens use. Apple notes that Wi-Fi and Bluetooth use the 2.4 GHz band and lists microwave ovens as a common source of interference.
Sources: [47 CFR 18.301](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/18.301) · [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-asia/102319)

## 3. Why the door mesh works
At 2.45 GHz the wave is about 12.2 cm long. The holes in the door's metal mesh are around a millimetre, much smaller than the wave, so the mesh blocks it while light still passes and you can see your food. Physicists call it "wave-guide below cutoff".
Sources: [Swinburne University, 23 Jul 2014](https://www.swinburne.edu.my/?p=4929) · [University of Illinois Physics Van, 22 Apr 2008](https://van.physics.illinois.edu/ask/listing/12217)

## 4. Almost all is not all (and that is safe)
The US standard lets an oven leak up to 5 mW per square centimetre at about 2 inches from the surface over its lifetime, far below levels known to harm people. At 20 inches it drops to about a hundredth of that.
Source: [FDA, current as of 12 Oct 2023](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens)

## 5. Why a tiny leak still wins
In the UK, 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi equipment is limited to 100 mW (a tenth of a watt). A household microwave is rated at roughly 750 to 1,100 W. Engineers report large drops in 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi throughput while an oven runs, usually worst on channels 6 to 11.
Sources: [UK SI 2009/1812](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/1812/made) · [Physics Factbook (US DOE figures)](https://hypertextbook.com/facts/2007/TatyanaNektalova.shtml) · [CWNP, 7 May 2013](https://cwnp.com/the-wise-article-series-interference-from-non-wifi-sources-part-1)

## 6. Even the regulator looked into it
A 2009 study for Ofcom named microwave ovens among the devices that can interfere with home Wi-Fi in the 2.4 GHz band (baby monitors and video senders were the bigger culprits).
Source: [ISPreview, 11 May 2009](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/story/2009/05/11/ofcom-survey-highlights-interference-problems-for-uk-wireless-networks.html)

## 7. What to do tonight
- Connect your phone and laptop to the 5 GHz (or 6 GHz) network if your router offers one.
- If you must stay on 2.4 GHz, try channel 1 and keep the router a few metres from the microwave.
- A microwave that is damaged around the door, hinges or seal should be checked or replaced.

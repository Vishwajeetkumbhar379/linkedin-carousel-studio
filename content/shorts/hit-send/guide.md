# What happens when you hit send on ChatGPT: the 1-page guide

Read this in 3 minutes and you will know why AI guesses, why it costs so much, and how to prompt it better.
Sources linked under every point, checked 23 Oct 2026 · @buildwithvish · October 2026

## 1. The short version
Your message is cut into tokens, sent to chips in a data centre, and the model predicts the reply one token at a time. It never looks the answer up. It guesses the most likely next piece, billions of times a day.

## 2. Tokens
A token is about three quarters of an English word. "Can you rewrite this email" is around 6 tokens. You pay, and the model thinks, in tokens.
Source: [OpenAI, what are tokens](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/4936856-what-are-tokens-and-how-to-count-them)

## 3. The chips
A single top data-centre AI chip costs more than $30,000, about the price of a car.
Source: [CNBC, 19 Mar 2024](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/03/19/nvidias-blackwell-ai-chip-will-cost-more-than-30000-ceo-says.html)

## 4. It guesses
The model scores every possible next token and picks one, then repeats. That is why it can sound sure and still be wrong.

## 5. The energy
Sam Altman says an average ChatGPT query uses about 0.34 Wh, roughly a high-efficiency light bulb for a couple of minutes. ChatGPT handles over 2.5 billion prompts a day (reported July 2025).
Sources: [Simon Willison on Altman, 10 Jun 2025](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/10/sam-altman/) · [Gigazine, 22 Jul 2025](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250722-chatgpt-prompts)

## 6. Please and thank you
Altman said polite words cost OpenAI "tens of millions of dollars well spent".
Source: [TechRadar, 20 Apr 2025](https://www.techradar.com/computing/artificial-intelligence/chatgpt-spends-tens-of-millions-of-dollars-on-people-playing-please-and-thank-you-but-sam-altman-says-its-worth-it)

## 7. Use it better
- Give it the facts to work from (paste the email, the data). Guessing from your facts beats guessing from memory.
- Ask for sources and check one.
- Short, specific prompts cost fewer tokens and usually get better answers.

# Is your phone listening to you? The 1-page guide

Read this in 3 minutes and you will know who claimed phones listen for ads, what the regulator found, and what really makes ads feel creepy.
Sources linked under every point, checked 28 Oct 2026 · @buildwithvish · October 2026

## 1. The short version
A US marketing firm told advertisers it could target ads from what people say near their devices. In 2026 the US Federal Trade Commission said the service never listened at all: it resold email lists from data brokers. Your phone doesn't need your voice to show you that ad.

## 2. The claim: "Active Listening"
In December 2023, 404 Media reported that a Cox Media Group (CMG) marketing unit was pitching "Active Listening": ads targeted from what people say out loud near device microphones. In August 2024, 404 Media published the pitch deck.
Sources: [404 Media, 14 Dec 2023](https://404media.co/cmg-cox-media-actually-listening-to-phones-smartspeakers-for-ads-marketing/) · [404 Media, 26 Aug 2024](https://www.404media.co/heres-the-pitch-deck-for-active-listening-ad-targeting/)

## 3. How the big platforms reacted
After 404 Media asked for comment, Google removed CMG from its advertising Partners Program. Meta said: "Meta does not use your phone's microphone for ads and we've been public about this for years." Amazon Ads said it had never worked with CMG on this programme.
Sources: [404 Media, 26 Aug 2024](https://www.404media.co/heres-the-pitch-deck-for-active-listening-ad-targeting/) · [New York Sun, 3 Sep 2024](https://nysun.com/article/in-new-leak-facebook-partner-brags-about-listening-to-phone-conversations-to-serve-up-ads-report) · [Facebook newsroom, 2 Jun 2016](https://about.fb.com/news/h/facebook-does-not-use-your-phones-microphone-for-ads-or-news-feed-stories/)

## 4. What the FTC found inside
The FTC says the service "did not, in fact, listen in on consumers' conversations or use voice data at all". Instead it "consisted of reselling, at a significant markup, email lists obtained from other data brokers". Consumers had not opted in either. CMG pays $880,000, MindSift and 1010 Digital Works $25,000 each: $930,000 in total. The orders were finalised on 27 August 2026.
Sources: [FTC press release, 21 May 2026](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-require-cox-media-group-two-other-firms-pay-nearly-1-million-settle-charges-they-deceived) · [FTC case page, 27 Aug 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/cmg-media-corporation-matter-timeline-item-2026-08-27)

## 5. What researchers found in 17,000 apps
Northeastern University researchers tested more than 17,000 popular Android apps. "Not a single app activated the microphone." What they did find: some apps sent screenshots and screen recordings of what you do to third parties.
Source: [Northeastern University, 6 Jul 2018](https://news.northeastern.edu/2018/07/06/is-your-smartphone-spying-on-you/)

## 6. So why does the ad feel like it heard you?
Ad systems already know your searches, your social activity and who you are connected to, and they predict what you want next. Friends talk about the same things they search for, and you remember the one ad that matched, not the thousands that didn't.
Source: [ProperData (NSF project), David Choffnes, Northeastern](https://properdata.eng.uci.edu/?p=6723)

## 7. What you can do today
- Check which apps have microphone access (iPhone: Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone; Android: Settings > Security & privacy > Privacy > Permission manager > Microphone) and switch off any that don't need it.
- Turn off ad personalisation in your Google and Meta ad settings.
- Limit location access to "while using the app" and review apps that track you across other apps.

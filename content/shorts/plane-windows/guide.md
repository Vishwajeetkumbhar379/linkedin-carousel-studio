# Why are plane windows round? The 1-page guide to the Comet disasters

Read this in 3 minutes and you will know why the world's first jet airliner kept breaking apart, what investigators found, and why every window you look out of today has rounded corners.
Sources linked under every point, checked 28 Oct 2026 · @buildwithvish · October 2026

## 1. The short version
In 1954 two de Havilland Comets, the first jet airliners, broke up in mid-air. Investigators traced it to metal fatigue: every flight the pressurised cabin swells and relaxes, and the stress piled up at the corners of squarish cut-outs in the skin until a crack ran. Windows were changed to the now-standard oval shape.
Source: [FAA Lessons Learned: de Havilland Comet 1](https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/G-ALYV)

## 2. The world's first jet airliner
BOAC started the first scheduled jet passenger service on 2 May 1952, London to Johannesburg, with Comet 1 G-ALYP.
Sources: [Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/first-aircraft-commercial-jet-powered) · [This Day in Aviation, 2 May 1952](https://www.thisdayinaviation.com/2-1952/)

## 3. Two break-ups in three months
On 10 January 1954 that same aircraft, G-ALYP (BOAC Flight 781), broke up off Elba, killing all 35 on board. On 8 April 1954 G-ALYY (South African Airways Flight 201) broke up near Naples, killing all 21. The Comet's certificate of airworthiness was withdrawn and the fleet grounded.
Sources: [BOAC Flight 781](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BOAC_Flight_781) · [South African Airways Flight 201](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Airways_Flight_201)

## 4. Why corners matter
A pressurised fuselage is pushed outwards on every flight. The stress flows around any hole in the skin. Around a curved edge it flows on with little build-up; at an abrupt corner it cannot, so it concentrates. The Comet's squarish windows created stress concentrations "much higher than anticipated".
Source: [FAA Lessons Learned](https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/G-ALYV)

## 5. The water-tank test
Investigators sank Comet G-ALYU in a water tank and pressurised it again and again. After 1,230 real pressurised flights plus 1,830 tank "flights" (about 3,060 in all), the fuselage failed at the corner of a squarish forward escape hatch window.
Source: [FAA Lessons Learned](https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/G-ALYV)

## 6. 16,000 versus 1,290
De Havilland's test fuselage had survived 16,000 pressure cycles, but it had been strengthened without anyone realising by earlier overpressure tests. Production Comets formed fatigue cracks near window corners at about 1,000 cycles. The Elba aircraft had made 1,290 pressurised flights, the Naples aircraft 900.
Source: [FAA Lessons Learned](https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/G-ALYV)

## 7. It started at a window in the roof
The first fracture on the Elba aircraft was in the piece of roof holding the two squarish ADF (radio direction finder) windows. The 1955 Court of Inquiry found the accident "was caused by structural failure of the pressure cabin in the region of the A.D.F. window, brought about by fatigue". So it was not a passenger window, but it was a square cut-out.
Sources: [FAA Lessons Learned](https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/G-ALYV) · [BOAC Flight 781 (Court of Inquiry report, 1 Feb 1955)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BOAC_Flight_781)

POST 1 (Tuesday)

A brand once made us redo one creator shortlist 22 times.

The brief: creators with a strong personal brand. Original. Nobody copying anyone.

We worked through the night to hit the deadline. The final list was niche and genuinely original.

Then the videos came in.

And the brand rewrote every line the creators said.

They wanted originality. They didn't want to give the freedom that creates it.

I've worked on 850+ creator deals from the brand side. I've seen this more than once.

If you hire a creator for their voice, let them use it. Brief the goal, not the script.

Brands: what's the hardest part of letting go?


POST 2 (Wednesday)

One meeting kept me stuck in India for 6 months.

I had worked remotely for about 4 years. My next two flights were booked.

Then an important meeting came up. I cancelled both flights. More client work followed. I ended up in a 9 to 5 for half a year.

For someone obsessed with travel, that was hell.

It taught me two things. Remote isn't freedom when:
1. you're paid in a currency that doesn't stretch
2. one meeting can call you back

So I'm changing both. I moved to Germany for my master's, and I'm building income in euros. In December I work from Sri Lanka for a month.

When I travel, I count every single day. When I don't, life runs on autopilot.

What's keeping you in one place right now?


POST 3 (Thursday)

The biggest hustle now is figuring out how not to hustle.

AI won't replace marketers.
Marketers who use AI will hand it the heavy lifting: the repetitive tool work nobody misses.

What's left is the part that was always the job.
Psychology. Taste. Creativity.

My daily stack: Claude Code, Claude, ChatGPT, ElevenLabs, Gemini.

I'm a marketer, not an engineer. I still built my own content system with them.

What's one task you'd hand to AI tomorrow?

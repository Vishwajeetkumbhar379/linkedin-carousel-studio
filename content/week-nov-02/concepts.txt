2. THE LESSONS THAT MATTER
[V PDF] Make the first 70 to 80% coherent and believable, then let ONE late detail change the meaning. Don't explain the joke.
[V PDF] Seven beats: ordinary frustration, plausible solution, polished demo, repeat (teach the rule), personal exception,
        re-score the emotion (music or silence), hold and leave.
[V PDF] Delete-the-effects test: read the script aloud with no music or visuals. If a stranger can't explain the twist, fix the
        story, not the motion. One extra clean sentence beats five stronger transitions.
[V PDF] At the reversal, choose ONE audio action (pitch drop, filter, hard cut or silence). Then hold. Test 0.6, 1.1, 1.7 s.
[V PDF] Narration: short opener, pause while the picture works, clean escalation, near-flat reaction to the reveal. The hardest
        beat may be deciding NOT to say the last line.
[V PDF] Humour engines: serious UI meets a private disaster; false convenience; automation that says what a polite friend
        wouldn't; unreliable metrics (the KPI that measures feelings); bureaucratic absurdity; silence as the punchline.
[V Berger & Milkman] Positive content is shared more than negative, and high-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety) are
        shared more than low-arousal ones like sadness. So: aim for surprise, amusement and awe, not sadness.
[R] Paritosh's strongest titles use a statement, a number and a time box; questions were weaker.
[R] Kallaway: name the topic in about five words, then turn it the other way. Proof on screen, not just talk.
[K] Loewenstein: curiosity needs a specific gap the viewer feels close to closing. Vague mystery doesn't work. Always pay it off.
[K] Green and Brock: people get absorbed through focus, mental pictures and feeling. So use concrete images ("one coffee, 3.50").
[K] Stanton: make me care in the first beat; give 2 + 2, not 4 (let the viewer work it out); know the ending first.
[K] Matthew Dicks: every story is about a five-second moment of change. Start at the opposite of where you end. Cut what doesn't serve that moment.
[K] Jenny Hoyos: write the ending first; a hook you understand with the sound off; stakes plus irony; end the moment the story ends.

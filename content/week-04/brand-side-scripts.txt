1. "Brands don't check your followers first."
ON SCREEN: FOLLOWERS? NOT FIRST.

I've been on the brand side of more than 850 creator deals.
And followers were never the first thing we checked.
First: your last ten posts. Do people actually comment, or is it just emojis?
Second: who's watching. Is your audience the one we're selling to?
Third: would our product look natural in your video, or forced?

3. "We redid one shortlist 22 times."
ON SCREEN: REDO #22

A brand once made us redo one creator shortlist twenty two times.
They wanted original creators. Strong personal brands. Nobody copying anybody.
We worked through the night and found them.
Then the brand rewrote every line those creators said.
Here's the lesson for creators.
Before you sign, ask one question: how much creative freedom do I get?
If the answer is "none", price it like an ad, not like content.

4. "The deal is not the post. It's the usage rights."
ON SCREEN: USAGE RIGHTS

Most creators price the post. Brands care about the usage.
Usage rights mean the brand can use your video as an ad. On their page. In paid campaigns. For months.
Your post might get ten thousand views. Their ad can get millions.
So here's the rule. The post has one price. Usage is extra, and it has an end date.
Thirty days, ninety days, never "forever" for one fee.
[How usage changed a budget in a deal you saw.]

5. "Never give your price first."
ON SCREEN: DON'T SAY A NUMBER

A brand asks: "What's your rate?"
Most creators panic and say a number. Usually too low.
Here's what I wish more creators said:
"Happy to share a quote. What's the budget, and what's the scope? Posts, stories, usage, exclusivity?"
Because every brand has a budget before they message you. I know, I set them.
Let them show their number first.

6. "Exclusivity should double your rate."
ON SCREEN: EXCLUSIVE = PAY MORE

"Exclusive for three months." Sounds fine. It isn't free.
Exclusivity means you can't work with their competitors.
So if you're a skincare creator and you sign exclusive with one skincare brand, you just turned down every other skincare deal.
That's money you lose. So the brand pays for it.
[What exclusivity usually looked like in the deals you saw.]
Short window, clear category, extra fee.
Save this for your next contract.

7. "Read this clause before you sign."
ON SCREEN: UNLIMITED REVISIONS

One word in a creator contract can eat your whole month: unlimited.
Unlimited revisions. Unlimited usage. Unlimited time.
Here's what to ask for instead.
Two rounds of revisions. Usage with an end date. Payment within thirty days of posting.
Brands expect you to push back. The good ones respect it.
[A time a creator's pushback made you trust them more.]

8. "The DM that got answered."
ON SCREEN: DM: IGNORED vs ANSWERED

Brands get hundreds of creator DMs. Here's why most get ignored.
"Hi, love your brand, let's collab!" Ignored. It's about you.
Here's the one that gets answered:
"Your new launch targets [audience]. My audience is exactly that. Here's a video idea for it in my style."
It's about them. It's specific. It's ready to go.
And send it to the marketing manager, not the brand account.

9. "What a brand manager actually wants."
ON SCREEN: THEIR KPI

Want to get rebooked? Make the brand manager look good to their boss.
Every brand manager has numbers they report. Reach. Clicks. Sales. Content they can reuse.
[What you had to report after a campaign.]
So after your post, send them a short recap. Views, saves, comments, and the best three comments.

10. "Your highlights are your CV."
ON SCREEN: EMPTY HIGHLIGHTS = NO DEAL

When a brand checks your profile, they look at three things in about three seconds.
Your bio: who's this for?
Your pinned posts: your best work.
Your highlights: proof. Past collabs, reviews, results.
Empty highlights look like no experience.
So make one highlight called "Work with me". Even one gifted collab counts.

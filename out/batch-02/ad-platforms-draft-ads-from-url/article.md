# Google, X and Meta now draft ads from your URL and photos: how to test them without wasting budget

## Three launches, one pattern

In the space of a week, three ad platforms showed tools that draft ads from what you already have.

**Google (1 October).** In an Ads Decoded post on Demand Gen and YouTube ads, Google said Asset Studio's multimodal video creation, powered by Gemini Omni, turns your website URLs and photos into high-quality videos that stakeholders can preview before you publish.

**X (6 October).** At Advertising Week New York, X launched **X Lift**. You enter your URL, X's AI generates ad options including images and copy, and it can build the whole campaign, targeting included. You set the budget. Social Media Today notes the main audience is new advertisers, and X reported that active self-serve advertisers grew 50% in Q3 2026 compared with Q3 2025.

**Meta (6 October).** Meta said advertisers can now generate video from existing static assets in Advantage+ creative, and that the feature is generally available. In Ads Creative Studio, marketers can generate video concepts and edit them to change hooks, adjust audio and clip length, add text overlays and captions, or change end scenes. Meta is also adding the ability for its business assistant to update targeting and change budgets through conversational prompts.

## Why this matters

The input is shifting. For years the creative brief was a document. Now, for a lot of small advertisers, the brief is the landing page and the assets already sitting in your folder. If the page is clear, these tools have something good to work with. If it's vague, you'll get a vague ad, just faster.

## How to test it without wasting budget

1. **Fix the page first.** Offer, price and proof near the top. That's what the AI will read.
2. **Start with what you have.** Google's own advice is that existing social assets are a solid base, as long as they follow its asset specs.
3. **Run AI drafts against your best current ad.** Same budget, same audience, one variable.
4. **Change things gradually.** Google recommends focusing on quality over quantity, so the system has time to measure.
5. **Check before anything goes live.** Every claim true, price and offer matching the page, no made-up reviews or numbers, and it still sounds like your brand.
6. **Keep a human sign-off on spend.** Especially as assistants gain the power to change budgets and targeting by prompt.

## The catch

X Lift can build the whole campaign, which means trusting X's system with targeting as well as creative. Meta's assistant is moving the same way. Automation is great at producing options. Deciding which option is true to your brand and worth the money is still your job.

## What I'd do

My rule from creator marketing carries straight over: a clear brief beats a clever tool. So I'd treat the landing page as the brief now. I'd rewrite it for one audience, one offer and one proof point, then let one of these tools draft against it, and compare the result to my best existing ad for two weeks before scaling anything.

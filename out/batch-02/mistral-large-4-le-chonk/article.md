# Mistral Large 4: Europe's 1 trillion parameter model, and what marketers can do with it

## What Mistral released

On 6 October Mistral launched a public preview of **Mistral Large 4**, or ML4. Its very official nickname, according to Mistral, is "le Chonk". Mistral describes it as a 1 trillion-parameter, natively multimodal model with 49 billion active parameters, and says it's the company's largest and most capable model to date.

You can try the preview API on Mistral Studio today. Mistral says it will release the weights by the end of the month. TechCrunch reported that, for now, it's only reachable through a public guardrail endpoint while safety testing finishes.

## The parts that matter for marketers

**Languages.** Mistral says a significant share of ML4's training data was multilingual, spanning more than 160 languages, including every official language of the European Union. If you run campaigns across several markets, that's the headline for you.

**Where it runs.** Mistral says ML4 was trained from scratch on 3,800 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GPUs in its own datacentres in Europe, and that it will offer a European deployment it operates end to end under European law. For clients who ask where their data is processed, that's a real answer.

**Deliverables.** Mistral says ML4 is strong on the professional work people actually hand over: spreadsheets, slides and PDFs. On AA-Briefcase, a benchmark of long-horizon knowledge work, it reports 1,393 Elo.

## The catch

It's a preview. The open weights, which are the reason many teams care about Mistral, aren't released yet. Benchmarks in the launch post are Mistral's own claims or tests it chose to report. And "trained on 160+ languages" isn't the same as "writes great German ad copy". You still have to check.

## How to try it, step by step

1. **Pick one real brief.** Not a toy prompt. A campaign brief you've already shipped, so you know what good looks like.
2. **Ask for localised versions.** For example: "Rewrite this brief for our German and French markets. Keep every claim. Flag anything that needs a local legal or cultural check."
3. **Have a native speaker judge it.** Blind, next to your existing human version.
4. **Compare cost and speed** against the model you use today.
5. **Decide later.** If the weights land as promised, you'll have the option to run it yourself.

## What I'd do

From where I sit in Germany, multilingual work is normal, not a special project. I'd run steps 1 to 3 on one German brief this week and keep a short log of what it got right and wrong. If it holds up, a European model with open weights could be a sensible default for client work where data location matters. If not, nothing lost but an hour.

# OpenAI launched Dots: always-on ChatGPT agents with their own computers

## OpenAI's Dots: agents that don't wait for you

At DevDay on 29 September, OpenAI launched **Dots**, a set of always-on AI agents inside ChatGPT. The idea is simple: instead of prompting ChatGPT one task at a time, you give an agent a goal and it keeps moving the work forward.

## How Dots work

Based on OpenAI's announcement and SiliconANGLE's coverage:

- **Powered by GPT-6 Astra.**
- **Its own cloud computer.** Each agent works on a machine you can open at any time to see what it is doing.
- **4,000+ apps.** More than 4,000 apps are reachable through OpenAI's plugins. With permission, an agent can also work directly on your laptop.
- **Where you talk to it.** ChatGPT on desktop, web or mobile, plus Slack and Teams. Context follows the agent between channels.
- **It learns your preferences** from feedback, and OpenAI says some work will show up before anyone asks for it.

## The safety rails

When nobody has given it a task, a Dot does what OpenAI calls proactive research. During that time, its app connections are read-only, so it can't send messages or change anything. Actions that could touch your accounts or share information must pass a check called auto-review. And OpenAI itself tells users to review consequential work because the agents "can still make mistakes."

One example in the coverage: an early tester had forgotten to bill a client. His agent noticed, prepared the invoice and waited for approval before sending it. That's the pattern to copy. Agent prepares, human approves.

## Who gets it, and who doesn't

Dots began rolling out to ChatGPT **Pro and Business Premium** subscribers. One agent is included at no extra cost. More agents and more capacity are planned later, at prices OpenAI hasn't disclosed.

The catch for my European readers: **Pro subscribers in the European Economic Area, Switzerland and the UK are not included.** Enterprise customers can try a beta once an admin switches it on.

## Why marketers should care

Most marketing work is a chain of small, repeatable steps: pull the numbers, spot the change, draft the update, chase the approval. That's exactly the shape of work an always-on agent is built for. The question isn't whether agents will touch marketing ops. It's whether your processes are written down clearly enough for one to follow.

## What I'd do

- **Write the job description first.** One goal, the apps it can touch, and what it must never do alone.
- **Start read-only.** Reporting and research before anything that sends, posts or spends.
- **Define approval points.** Anything customer-facing or money-related gets a human check. Always.
- **Pick a boring task.** Weekly campaign summaries are a better first job than brand strategy.
- **If you're in Europe,** use the waiting time to document your workflows. That's the work that makes agents useful later.

I'm honest about my limit here: I haven't used Dots myself yet. This is based on the launch details, not hands-on testing.

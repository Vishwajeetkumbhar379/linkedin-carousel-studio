# YouTube Custom Feeds: viewers will prompt their own homepage

## YouTube is handing viewers the remote for their homepage

At **Made On YouTube 2026** on 23 September, YouTube announced more than a few AI features. For marketers, one stands out: **Custom Feeds**.

## What Custom Feeds are

According to YouTube's official blog, Custom Feeds will let users in the US "curate your homepage based on specific moods or interests." It's coming to web, mobile and TVs this fall.

Social Media Today described how it works: a viewer creates a feed with a prompt, and that feed then lives as a tab at the top of their homepage. So someone could have a tab for quick dinner recipes, another for marathon training, another for B2B marketing breakdowns.

YouTube also highlighted Ask YouTube for instant recommendations. Put together, the direction is clear: viewers are getting more ways to say what they want in plain language.

## Why this changes the job

Until now, the YouTube homepage has been one feed per person, shaped mostly by what they watched before. You competed to look like the next sensible video.

With Custom Feeds, a viewer can describe an intent directly. That shifts the question from "does my video resemble what this person watched?" to "does my video clearly match what this person asked for?"

That's a familiar shift for anyone who has followed AI search. When a prompt sorts the content, clarity about topic and audience matters more.

## What we don't know

I want to be honest about the limits. YouTube hasn't explained how Custom Feeds choose videos, what signals they rely on, or whether ads appear inside them. It's US only at first. So anyone telling you exactly how to "rank in Custom Feeds" today is guessing.

What we can say is that a video with a vague title and an empty description gives any system less to work with.

## What I'd do

- **Say who the video is for, early.** In the title, the first line of the description and the first seconds of the video.
- **Name the problem in plain words.** The words a viewer would type, not your internal campaign name.
- **Use chapters.** They describe what's inside the video in a structured way.
- **Build series around clear interests.** A playlist on one topic is easier to match to a mood or interest than a random mix.
- **Watch your traffic sources.** When Custom Feeds roll out, check YouTube Analytics for changes in where views come from, before changing strategy.

From my creator marketing work, the channels that held up through every algorithm change were the ones with a clear promise. Custom Feeds look like another reward for that.

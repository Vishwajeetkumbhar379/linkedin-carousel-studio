YouTube viewers will soon build their own homepage.

At Made On YouTube, the platform announced Custom Feeds:
→ viewers describe a mood or interest
→ YouTube builds a feed for it
→ it sits as a tab at the top of their homepage
→ coming to the US this autumn on web, mobile and TV

Why this matters for marketers: the homepage used to be one feed, shaped by what you watched. Now people can ask for what they want, in their own words.

That changes the brief for every video. It's not enough to be similar to last week's viewing. Your video has to clearly match what someone might ask for.

My practical takeaway: write titles and descriptions that say exactly who the video is for and what problem it solves. Clear beats clever when a prompt is doing the sorting.

I'll admit we don't know yet how Custom Feeds pick videos. But clarity has never hurt discovery.

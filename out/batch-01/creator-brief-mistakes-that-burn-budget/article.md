# Five creator brief mistakes that quietly burn budget

When a creator campaign goes over budget, people usually blame the creator: late drafts, off-brief content, a reshoot. Across 850+ creator deals, I've found the cause is more often upstream. It's in the brief.

None of these mistakes look expensive on the day you send the brief. They show up weeks later as a reshoot, a missed posting date, or a video that sounds like an ad and performs like one. I've made some of these myself.

## 1. Writing the script for them

A word-for-word script feels safe. It isn't. You hired a creator for the way they talk to their audience, and a script takes that away. Viewers can tell when someone is reading brand copy, and they scroll.

**Fix:** write "the one thing to get across" and two or three must-include points. Let the creator say it in their own words. If you need a script for a paid ad, that's a different job, and should be briefed and paid as one.

## 2. Six key messages

Brands have a lot to say. A short video can carry one message well, maybe two. Ask for six and you'll get a rushed list that nobody remembers, or a creator who picks one and leaves your favourite out.

**Fix:** choose one message. Put everything else on the landing page, where people who are interested will read it.

## 3. No approved claims list

"Talk about how good it is for your skin" sounds harmless until legal sees the draft and every claim needs rewording. That's the most expensive time to change anything: the creator has filmed, edited and planned their posting date.

**Fix:** list the exact approved claims in the brief. Add a line on claims to avoid. If you're working across Europe, add the disclosure label too. My [ad labels guide](https://buildwithvish.netlify.app/#read-ad-labels-in-europe) covers the basics.

## 4. Feedback from everyone

The brand manager likes the draft. Then the product team, the regional lead and someone from legal each add notes, some of which contradict each other. The creator gets a long list of edits and goes into round three.

**Fix:** name one approver in the brief. Collect internal feedback before it reaches the creator. Cap feedback at two rounds, and say so in writing.

## 5. Hiding the real plan

If you plan to put paid budget behind the content, say so. Creators film differently for paid use: a strong opening line, cleaner cutdowns, nothing that dates the video quickly. Find out later, and you'll pay for edits, or for usage rights you didn't price in.

The same goes for the real KPI. If the goal is clicks, not views, tell them. They'll structure the video around it.

**Fix:** put usage (organic, paid, how long, where) and the main KPI in the brief, and in the contract. My [contract red flags guide](https://buildwithvish.netlify.app/#read-creator-contract-red-flags) covers the clauses to check.

## Use AI to check your brief

Before sending, paste your brief into any AI chat with this:

```
Review this creator brief as an experienced creator would.
Flag: (1) any scripted lines that would sound unnatural, (2) more than 2 key messages,
(3) missing approved claims, (4) unclear approval process, (5) missing usage or KPI.
For each issue, quote the line and suggest a one-line fix.
Brief: [paste]
```

It won't catch everything, but it catches the obvious ones before a creator has to.

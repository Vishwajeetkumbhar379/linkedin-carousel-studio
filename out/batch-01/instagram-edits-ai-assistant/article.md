# Instagram's Edits app now has an AI assistant that reads your Reels data

## Instagram's Edits app now reads your numbers for you

On 30 September, Instagram announced an AI video assistant inside **Edits**, its video editing app. It's a chat assistant, but the selling point isn't editing. It's analysis.

## What the Edits assistant does

Brett Westervelt, who leads Edits, described it like this: the assistant knows your account's Instagram metrics, "things like follows, views, video retention, likes, shares," and combines them with your comments, what's trending on Instagram and what your audience is into. He said it can spot patterns in performance over time and surface insights that are hard to see from the metrics alone.

From there, it suggests what to make next. PetaPixel summed it up as an assistant that analyses your Reels and suggests new ideas.

## The pitch: analysis, not authorship

The most interesting line from Instagram is about what the tool won't do. Westervelt said creators consistently told the team they want "a tool that handles the analysis, not one that does the creative work for them." His summary: "Edits assistant does the digging for you. The creative calls are still yours."

That matches what LinkedIn did with its proofreader. Platforms are learning that audiences push back on content that feels generated. So the AI is moving behind the scenes, into data and planning.

## The catch

- **It's capped.** Creators hit a usage limit and can get more with a Meta One subscription.
- **It only sees Instagram.** The assistant works from your Instagram data. Your TikTok or YouTube performance isn't part of the picture.
- **Suggestions are still guesses.** Patterns in past data don't guarantee the next video works. Treat ideas as hypotheses to test.

Meta first previewed the assistant in June at an invite-only creator event, and TechCrunch notes YouTube is working on its own conversational editing tool for early next year. Expect this to become standard.

## Why this matters for brands

Having worked on 850+ creator deals, I'd always rather see a creator's retention and share data than a follower count. Followers tell you reach. Retention tells you whether people actually watched.

If creators get an assistant that turns their own stats into clear insight, two things change:

1. Creator pitches get sharper, with reasons for each content idea.
2. Brand briefs that ignore the creator's data will look lazy by comparison.

## What I'd do

- **Creators:** ask the assistant one question a week: "Which of my last 10 Reels kept people watching longest, and what did they have in common?" Then make one video that tests the answer.
- **Brands and agencies:** ask creators to share what their assistant suggests before you send a brief. Build the brief around their audience, not your mood board.
- **Everyone:** keep the creative call human. Use the data to choose the topic. Use your taste to make it.

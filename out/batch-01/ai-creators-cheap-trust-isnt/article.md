# AI avatars can make the ad. They can't make people trust it

AI avatar ads keep getting better. A brand can now produce a person-shaped video without a contract, a product shipment, a draft deadline or a reshoot. After 850+ creator deals, I understand the appeal better than most. Chasing approvals, rescheduling shoots and waiting on drafts is real work.

But I think a lot of brands are mixing up two different things: the *ad* and the *trust behind it*. AI can make the first. It can't make the second.

This is an opinion piece, from my experience on the creator side. No statistics.

## What you're actually paying a creator for

When you pay a creator, you aren't really paying for a face reading your script. You're paying for:

- **An audience that chose them.** People followed for a reason, and keep watching for the same reason.
- **A track record.** Years of posts, opinions, recommendations, and mistakes, out in public.
- **Their "no".** The brands they turned down are why their "yes" means something.
- **Their comment section.** Real people asking real questions, and the creator answering.

That's where belief comes from. The video is just the delivery.

## What an avatar doesn't have

An AI avatar can look and sound convincing. But it has:

- no audience that chose it
- no history of recommendations to judge it by
- no record of ever turning anything down
- no reputation at stake if the product disappoints

So when an avatar says "I love this product", nobody is actually saying it. Viewers may not consciously notice. But I'd bet that over time, trust settles where there's someone behind the words.

## Where I would use AI avatars

I'm not against them. There are good uses:

- **Testing angles cheaply.** Run five hooks with an avatar first, then brief creators with the angle that worked.
- **Explainers and how-tos**, where the viewer needs information, not a personal recommendation.
- **Internal content**: training, onboarding, product updates.
- **Localising** content you already own, where rules and consent allow it.

In all of these, the viewer doesn't need to believe a person. They need to understand something.

## Where I'd still pay a person

Anything where the viewer has to trust the recommendation:

- a product someone will put on their skin or feed their kids
- a purchase that's expensive or hard to return
- a category full of scepticism, like finance, health or supplements
- a launch where comments and questions are part of the plan

## Label it, whatever you choose

If you use an AI avatar in an ad, make it obvious. Hiding it borrows trust you didn't earn, and when people find out, you lose the trust you did have. Check the current rules in each market before you launch. They are changing, and this isn't legal advice.

## What I'd do

- Split your budget by job: AI for testing and explaining, creators for recommending.
- Use avatar tests to write better creator briefs, not to replace the creators. The brief gets sharper, and the creator spends their trust on an angle that already works.
- Pick creators on fit, not just reach. My [shortlist rubric](https://buildwithvish.netlify.app/#read-shortlist-creators-with-ai) shows how.
- Ask yourself one question before choosing: does this viewer need to *understand*, or need to *believe*?

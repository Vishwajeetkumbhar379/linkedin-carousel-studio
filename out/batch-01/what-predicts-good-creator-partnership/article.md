# What actually predicts a good creator partnership

People ask me which number predicts a good creator partnership. Followers? Engagement rate? Average views? After 850+ creator deals, my honest answer is that the best early signal isn't a number at all. It's the creator's first reply.

This is opinion from experience, not a study. But it's the pattern I'd bet on.

## They ask questions before they quote

A good partner's first reply usually has questions in it:

- Who is this campaign for?
- What does it need to achieve?
- What can't I say?
- Is this organic only, or will it run as an ad?

A creator who asks these is already thinking about their audience and your goal at the same time. That's the whole job. A reply that's only a rate card isn't a red flag on its own, plenty of busy creators work that way. But the questions tell you how they'll treat the brief.

## They push back on the brief

Some of the best content I've seen came from a creator saying "this won't work for my audience, can I do it like this instead?"

That can feel uncomfortable on the brand side, especially if a client has signed off on a script. But creators usually know their audience better than we do. Pushback is a sign they care how it lands. A creator who agrees to everything may also deliver exactly what you asked for, and nothing better.

## They're honest about what flopped

Ask a creator "what kind of brand content hasn't worked for you?" The good ones answer straight away, with specifics. That honesty is worth more than a highlight reel. It tells you what to avoid and that they'll tell you the truth when the numbers come in.

## They've said no before

Creators who turn down products that don't fit their audience protect the thing you're paying for: trust. If someone has promoted every brand that asked, their audience has learnt to scroll past the sponsored posts. Their "no" is why people believe their "yes".

You can often spot this in their content. Fewer sponsored posts, and the ones that exist feel like they belong.

## They follow through on the boring bits

Drafts on time. Disclosure labels done right the first time. A reply within a working day or two. None of this is exciting, and all of it predicts whether you'll want to work together again.

## What doesn't predict much on its own

- **Follower count.** It tells you the ceiling, not what you'll get.
- **A polished media kit.** Useful, but it's marketing. Treat it like one.
- **One viral post.** Look at the typical post, not the best one.

## What I'd do

- Add three questions to your first outreach, and watch how creators answer: "What would make this work for your audience?", "What hasn't worked for you before?", "Anything you wouldn't say about a product?"
- Score fit before cost. My [creator shortlist rubric](https://buildwithvish.netlify.app/#read-shortlist-creators-with-ai) puts audience and content fit first for this reason.
- Leave room in the brief for pushback. Write "the one thing to get across", not a word-for-word script. My [one-page brief](https://buildwithvish.netlify.app/#read-one-page-creator-brief) shows how.
- After each campaign, note which early signals turned out right. Your own notes will beat any rule of thumb, including mine.

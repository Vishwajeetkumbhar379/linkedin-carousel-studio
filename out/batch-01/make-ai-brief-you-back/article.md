# Make AI brief you back before it writes a word

Most weak AI drafts I see aren't a model problem. They're a listening problem. The AI read your brief, missed what mattered, filled every gap with the most average guess, and then wrote 600 confident words in the wrong direction.

Agencies solved this long before AI. It's called the **brief-back**. When a client hands over a brief, the team doesn't start writing. They come back and say: "Here's what we heard. Here's what we think is missing. Here's what we're assuming." Only when both sides agree does the work start.

I've written hundreds of creator briefs, and the brief-back caught more problems than any revision round. So I now run it with AI on anything longer than a caption.

## Step 1: Paste the brief and stop it writing

Use whatever brief you have. If you use my [six-box brief](https://buildwithvish.netlify.app/#read-brief-ai-like-an-agency), even better. Then add this:

```
Don't write anything yet. Brief me back first.

1. WHAT I HEARD: the goal, audience and main message, in your own words, 3 lines max.
2. WHAT'S MISSING: anything you'd need to do this well that I haven't given you.
3. WHAT YOU'RE ASSUMING: every gap you'd fill with a guess. Label each guess.
4. SUCCESS TEST: how we'd know this piece worked, in one sentence.
5. ONE RISK: the most likely way this goes wrong.

Keep it short. Ask, don't fill.
```

## Step 2: Read it like a client, not a writer

This is the useful part. Look for three things:

- **A goal you didn't mean.** If it says "drive awareness" and you meant "get sign-ups", the whole draft would have been wrong.
- **Assumptions that are guesses about your audience.** These are where generic copy comes from.
- **A success test you can't measure.** If neither of you can say what "worked" means, nobody can judge the draft.

Honest bit: the first few times I did this, most of the gaps were mine. Vague goal, no audience detail, nothing about what to avoid. The AI didn't create those problems. It made them visible.

## Step 3: Fix the brief, not the draft

Answer its questions in the same chat. Correct anything it misheard. Then lock it:

```
Update the brief with my answers. Show me the final version in the same 5 parts.
```

If the final brief-back reads right, the draft usually will too. If it still feels off, fix it here. Rewriting a brief takes a minute. Rewriting three drafts takes an afternoon.

## Step 4: Write from the agreed brief only

```
Now write it. Use only what we agreed above.
If you need to assume anything new, list it at the end instead of guessing in the copy.
```

That last line matters. It stops new guesses sneaking into the copy and tells you exactly what to check.

## When to skip it

Not everything needs a brief-back. A quick caption or a subject line test, just ask. I use it for anything that goes to a client, a creator, or a boss: campaign briefs, landing page copy, scripts, reports.

## What I'd do

- Save the Step 1 prompt as a text snippet or inside a Claude or ChatGPT Project so it's one click.
- Run it on the next brief you'd normally just hand over, and count the gaps it finds.
- Use the same habit with people. Ask a freelancer or creator to brief you back in three lines before they start. It's polite, and it saves everyone a revision round.

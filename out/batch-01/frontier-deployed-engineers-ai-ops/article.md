# Anthropic is training 10,000 'Frontier Deployed Engineers'. What marketers should take from it

On 2 October 2026, Anthropic launched **Claude Frontier Academy**, backed by a **$100 million** commitment. Its aim is to train **10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers (FDEs) by the end of 2027**. Engineers from Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley and Novo Nordisk are in the first cohorts.

I'm not an engineer, and this programme isn't for me. But I'm moving from creator marketing into AI Ops, and I think the way Anthropic describes this job is worth a close look for anyone on a similar path.

## What Anthropic actually announced

According to Anthropic's announcement, the first programme is the **Frontier Deployed Engineer Residency**:

- It starts with a **multi-day, in-person programme** with Anthropic engineers and licensed instructors.
- Participants work through a **simulated enterprise deployment**, from choosing the right use case, through security review, to handover, and finish with a graded practical.
- Those who pass move into a **12-week residency**, leading a real Claude use case at their own organisation.
- It's for **hands-on software engineers**, and participation is **by nomination**. The first full badges are expected in **early 2027**.

Commonwealth Bank's own newsroom confirmed that first intakes run in San Francisco, New York and London in October and November 2026, and that an initial group of 20 CommBank engineers will do a four-day intensive before the residency.

## The shape of the job

Strip out the code and the role has three parts:

1. **Pick the right problem.** Which process is worth changing, and what would "better" mean?
2. **Get it through safely.** Security review, data access, compliance, sign-off.
3. **Make people actually use it.** Handover, training, adoption.

The engineering is what makes it possible. But two of those three parts are about judgement, process and people. That's the part I'd call AI Ops, and it's where I think marketers can grow.

## Why marketers are closer than they think

If you've run campaigns, you've already done versions of this:

- **Choosing the use case** is like choosing the campaign objective. Most failures start with the wrong one.
- **Security review** feels a lot like legal review of a creator contract or claims list. You learn which questions get asked, and you answer them before they're asked.
- **Adoption** is a marketing problem. A tool nobody uses is a launch with no audience.

I'm not saying a marketer can do an FDE's job. The building part is real engineering, and I'm still learning it. I'm saying the non-code half of AI rollout needs people who can brief, plan, persuade and measure. Those are marketing skills.

## What I'd do

If you're a marketer curious about AI Ops, I'd start here:

- **Run one internal AI project end to end.** Pick a small process (weekly reporting, brief writing), set a success test, get IT or legal sign-off, and track whether people still use it a month later.
- **Write it up like a deployment.** Problem, approach, risks, review, adoption. That document is your portfolio piece.
- **Learn enough of the technical side to be useful.** Projects, connectors, basic automation. My guide to [connectors and MCP](https://buildwithvish.netlify.app/#read-connectors-and-mcp) is a gentle start.
- **Read the job descriptions, not the hype.** Look at what AI rollout roles actually ask for, and map your experience to it honestly.

If you're an engineer who might get nominated, the residency sounds like a serious programme. If you're not, the lesson is in the structure: problem, safety, adoption. You can practise all three this month.

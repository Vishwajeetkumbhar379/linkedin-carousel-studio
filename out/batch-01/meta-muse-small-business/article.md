# Meta's Muse agent now plugs into small business shops, books and ad accounts

## Meta takes Muse to small businesses

On 29 September, Meta announced **Muse for Small Business**, a version of its Muse AI agent that connects to the software owners already use to run their companies. Muse itself launched on 8 September for users in the US and Canada as a personal assistant.

## What it connects to

According to Meta's announcement, as reported by The Next Web:

- **Commerce and money:** Shopify, Stripe and Intuit's QuickBooks, among others
- **Design:** Canva
- **Meta's own tools:** analytics from Instagram business accounts and Facebook Pages, plus Meta ad accounts
- **Work apps:** Slack, Zoom, Notion, Figma and Dropbox
- **Custom connectors** for services Meta doesn't support yet

With those connections, Muse can draw on a company's brand, shop, accounts and customer records. Reuters reported that owners can ask it to analyse sales, campaigns and social activity, develop a growth plan, draft customer communications, and create ads and other content.

## The approval rule

Meta said the agent will not post content, send messages or buy anything without the owner's approval. That's the most important line in the announcement. It turns Muse into a drafting and analysis assistant with a human sign-off, not an autopilot.

## Why Meta is doing this

Meta's Vishal Shah told Axios that roughly **one in three** people who tried Muse early had already connected it to a business account of some kind. "People are using Muse to run their business," he said. In other words, owners were already doing this informally. Meta is making it official.

Most people can use Muse at no cost, with paid plans offering higher usage limits.

## What it means for marketers

For a small business, the appeal is obvious: one assistant that can see your sales, your social stats and your ad account in the same conversation. The usual problem with small business marketing isn't a lack of tools. It's that nobody has time to join the dots between them.

The risk is just as obvious. An agent that drafts ads from your data will reflect the quality of your inputs. If your positioning is fuzzy, it will produce fuzzy ads at scale.

## What I'd do

- **Write a one-page brand brief first.** Who you sell to, what you promise, three words for your tone, and words you never use.
- **Start with analysis, not creation.** Ask it what changed in sales and campaigns this month before you ask it for new ads.
- **Keep approval as a habit.** Read every draft as if a junior wrote it. Because, in effect, one did.
- **Check what it can see.** Connect only the accounts it needs for the job.
- **Compare against a human version.** Run one AI-drafted ad next to one you wrote, and let the numbers decide.

My limit: Muse is available in the US and Canada, and I haven't tested the business version myself. These are the launch details, plus how I'd approach any new marketing agent.

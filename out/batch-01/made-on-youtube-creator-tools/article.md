# Made On YouTube 2026: the creator tools that matter for brands

## Made On YouTube 2026, filtered for creators and brands

On 23 September, YouTube announced its yearly batch of product updates at **Made On YouTube 2026**. There's a lot in there. Here are the five updates I think matter most for creators and the brands that work with them.

## 1. An AI editing assistant you talk to

YouTube is integrating Gemini into a **conversational editing assistant** for Shorts and the YouTube Create app. According to YouTube, creators can use text prompts to **trim speech, sync music, add text hooks and reorder frames**.

TechCrunch reports the tool should be available early next year. So this is coming, not here.

Why it matters: the boring parts of short-form editing are exactly the parts that slow creators down. If a prompt can tighten the first three seconds and add a hook, more creators can publish more often.

## 2. Shorts series

Creators can now structure Shorts into **seasons and episodes**, making episodic storytelling and microdramas easier to watch on mobile and TV.

For brands, that opens a format beyond the single sponsored video: a short branded series with a story that carries across episodes.

## 3. Likeness detection for face and voice

YouTube says creators can **protect their voice and face with expanded likeness detection**. As AI generated copies of real people get easier to make, this is becoming a practical safety tool, not a niche feature.

## 4. Getting noticed by brands

YouTube also highlighted ways for creators to **get noticed by brands and find resources for pitching partnerships**. Details are still emerging, but the direction is clear: YouTube wants more of the brand deal process to happen on YouTube.

## 5. Live auto-dubbing

Live streamers get **real-time auto-dubbing** to break language barriers. For creators with international audiences, a live Q&A or product launch could reach viewers who don't speak the creator's language.

## What it means for brand partnerships

From my work across 850+ creator deals, three practical changes follow:

- **Contracts need likeness terms.** Who can use the creator's face and voice, for how long, and whether AI versions are allowed. Write it down.
- **Briefs can ask for series, not one-offs.** A three-episode Shorts arc may tell your story better than one 60-second ad.
- **Production timelines may shrink.** If editing gets faster, plan for quicker turnarounds, but keep review steps.

## What I'd do

- Creators: register for likeness detection as soon as it's available to you.
- Brands: add an AI and likeness clause to your next creator contract.
- Both: sketch one Shorts series idea now, so you're ready when your audience starts watching in episodes.

My limit: several of these features roll out over the coming months, and some may arrive in the US first. Check availability in your market before planning around them.

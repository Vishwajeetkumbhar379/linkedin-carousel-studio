# Stop targeting kids with influencers: audit, filter and automate compliance

## The problem
Recent complaints show that influencer marketing can reach children even when the product is not meant for them. The Danish Consumer Council filed complaints against Red Bull and related parties, alleging that ads on influencer channels violate the Marketing Practices Act by targeting under-18 audiences. Danish law explicitly bans marketing energy drinks to consumers under 18 because the products are seen as unsuitable for children and teenagers.

## Why kids are at risk
Influencer content often blends entertainment with promotion, making it hard for young viewers to tell when they are seeing an ad. Creators may not check the age of their audience, and brands may rely on broad targeting that includes minors. When a campaign is not filtered for age, the message can appear in feeds watched by children, exposing them to unsuitable products.

## What I'd do
I would start by reviewing every creator contract for age-related clauses. Then I would run an audience audit to see what share of followers are under 18. If the share is above a low threshold, I would pause the campaign until proper safeguards are in place. Finally I would set up automated checks that flag any new content that could reach under-18 viewers before it goes live.

## How to audit
Ask the creator for demographic insights from their platform analytics. Look for the percentage of followers aged 13-17 (or younger if available). Compare that to the brand’s age limit for the product. If the number exceeds the limit, treat the partnership as non-compliant.

## How to filter
Use a simple rule: block any creator whose under-18 share is higher than the allowed limit. Maintain a whitelist of creators who pass the audit. Update the list regularly as follower bases change.

## How to automate compliance
Integrate an AI tool to scan upcoming posts for prohibited products and age-sensitive language. Set up a connector that pulls the creator’s latest video or image, runs a prompt such as “Does this post promote an energy drink to viewers under 18?” and returns a yes/no answer. If the answer is yes, hold the post for manual review. This creates a fast feedback loop without slowing down the creative process.
